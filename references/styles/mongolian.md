# Mongolian and Central Asian steppe music

Reach for it: steppe, horses, wind, distance, ancestral memory, journeys, a solitary rider, endurance, grief that is wide rather than deep.

## Melodic logic

- Anhemitonic pentatonic scales (no semitones): C D E G A, or minor-feeling C D F G A / C Eb F G Bb. Wide melodic leaps (fourths, fifths, octaves) are characteristic; the melody imitates the contour of hills and horse gait.
- **Urtyn duu** ("long song"): extremely slow, free-rhythm, each syllable stretched over many ornamented notes, with wide vibrato-like oscillations. This is the form for grief, distance and praise of the land. Few words, long time.
- **Bogino duu** ("short song"): strophic, metered, trotting rhythm, for narrative and humour.

## Throat singing (khoomei)

A single singer produces a low drone (the fundamental) and a whistled melody of overtones above it.

- **Sygyt**: clear, high whistle-like overtone melody; bright, daytime, birds and wind.
- **Kargyraa**: very low, growling undertone; night, earth, the oldest voices.
- **Khoomei proper**: mid-register, softer overtones, between the two.

Where to use it: the prelude (a drone that becomes a voice), or a wordless passage after the verse. In the generation prompt: "Mongolian throat singing, sygyt overtone whistle over a low drone" or "kargyraa low undertone". In a MIDI mock, render the drone as a low sustained tone and the overtone line as a soft, pure sine melody two octaves up.

## Rhythm

- Long song: no meter; mark the score "free, following breath".
- Short song and riding songs: 2/4 or 4/4 at a trot (90 to 120 BPM), often with a galloping dotted figure. Faster riding rhythms imitate hoofbeats; write them into the percussion or the fiddle's bow.

## Instruments

- **Morin khuur** (horsehead fiddle): two strings, bowed, the sound of wind over grass; carries drones, double stops, and the melody. The central instrument.
- **Tovshuur**: two-string lute, rhythmic strumming, epics.
- **Yatga**: zither, gentle, water and rain.
- **Limbe**: side-blown flute, circular breathing, long phrases.
- **Jaw harp** (khel khuur): overtone texture.
- Percussion: sparse; frame drum, hoof-like clicks, stones.

## Backdrop ambience

Wind (constant, low), distant horses, a river over stones, felt walls of a ger muffling the outside, the crackle of a dung fire.

## Fusion partners

Morin khuur with cello and a low synth drone; sygyt over a Carnatic tanpura; a long-song melody sung in another language entirely (the technique transfers to any text).

## What outsiders get wrong

- Making it loud and fast. The steppe idiom is mostly slow and vast.
- Harmonising the drone. One drone, one melody, overtones.
- Using khoomei as a novelty sting. Give it a full phrase and a reason.
- Mongolian music is living culture, not a sound effect: describe it accurately in the brief and credit the tradition.
