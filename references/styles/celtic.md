# Celtic and British-Irish folk

Reach for it: sea, cliffs, rain, homeland and leaving it, laments, pub warmth, a dance that fights the sorrow, a letter home.

## Modes

| Mode | Scale (from D) | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Dorian | D E F G A B C | melancholy with motion; the default for ballads |
| Mixolydian | D E F# G A B C | bright but rooted, pipes and reels |
| Aeolian | D E F G A Bb C | pure lament |
| Major | D E F# G A B C# | airs, love songs, hymn-like |
| Gapped pentatonic | D E G A B / D F G A C | oldest-sounding airs |

Common keys for instruments: D and G for fiddle and whistle, A and D for pipes. Keep accompaniment sparse: a drone (pipes or a held fiddle string), or open-voiced guitar in DADGAD, moving I - bVII - IV rather than functional progressions.

## Rhythms and forms

| Form | Meter | Tempo | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slow air | free or 3/4, 4/4 | 50 to 70 | lament, the prelude, the conclusion |
| Waltz | 3/4 | 100 to 130 | nostalgia, sway |
| Jig | 6/8 | 110 to 130 (dotted quarter) | lift, journey |
| Slip jig | 9/8 | | floating, dreamlike |
| Reel | 4/4 (felt in 2) | 100 to 120 (half note) | drive, dance, celebration |
| Hornpipe | 4/4 swung | 90 to 110 | swagger |
| Ballad | 4/4 or 3/4 | 60 to 90 | storytelling verses |

A classic shape for a grief text: slow air prelude, ballad verses, then a short jig or reel in the conclusion (the dance that refuses the sorrow), ending back on the air's last phrase.

## Singing

- **Sean-nós** (Irish, "old style"): unaccompanied or over a drone, free rhythm, dense ornament, no vibrato, the singer's eyes closed. For intimate laments.
- Ballad singing: plain, clear, storyteller's diction, little vibrato.
- Scots and Welsh traditions: Welsh hymn-singing is four-part and swelling; use it for a crowd.
- Ornaments: rolls, cuts, grace notes; in the prompt: "ornamented with rolls and cuts, no vibrato".

## Instruments

- **Fiddle** (lead, drones on open strings), **tin whistle** / **low whistle** (breathy, haunting), **uilleann pipes** (regulators give chords; Irish), **Highland pipes** (loud, outdoors; Scottish), **Irish flute** (wooden, warm), **harp** (Celtic harp, arpeggios, the oldest voice), **bouzouki** / **guitar** (DADGAD), **bodhrán** (frame drum, pulse), **concertina** / **accordion**.
- Ambience: surf, gulls, wind in rigging, rain on slate, a peat fire, a crowd in a bar.

## Fusion partners

Fiddle trading phrases with Carnatic violin (both slide); low whistle over a tabla dadra; a sean-nós line over a synth pad; bodhrán under a West African kora ostinato.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Over-harmonising with pop chords; keep drones and modal movement.
- Wide vibrato in sean-nós.
- "Celtic" as a vague mood; name the tradition (Irish, Scottish, Welsh, Breton, Galician) in the brief when the backdrop allows.
