# Jazz, blues, gospel and American roots (including country and folk)

Reach for it: night, cities, loss that is lived with rather than mourned, wit, a bar, a porch, a road; devotion sung by a congregation; a plain story told plainly.

## Blues

- 12-bar form: I I I I / IV IV I I / V IV I I, dominant 7th chords throughout. 8-bar and 16-bar variants exist.
- Blue notes: flattened 3rd, 5th and 7th, bent into. Scale: C Eb F F# G Bb.
- Call and response: the voice sings a line, the guitar or harmonica answers.
- Tempo: slow blues 60 to 75 (12/8 feel), shuffle 100 to 130.
- Instruments: acoustic or electric guitar with slide, harmonica, upright bass, brushed drums, piano. Delta blues is one voice and one guitar on a porch.

## Jazz

- ii - V - I is the engine; extensions (9, 11, 13), tritone substitutions, modal passages (Dorian, Lydian) for openness.
- Swing feel: eighth notes as long-short triplets. Ballads at 55 to 70, medium swing 120 to 160, bossa nova 120 to 140 straight.
- Forms: AABA 32-bar standard; a lyric text often fits an AABA with the turn line as the bridge (B).
- Instruments: piano trio (piano, upright bass, drums with brushes or ride), muted trumpet, tenor sax, vibraphone for late night, nylon guitar for bossa.
- Voice: conversational phrasing behind the beat, little vibrato until the note's end, intimate microphone closeness.

## Gospel

- Major key with IV - I plagal movement, secondary dominants, chromatic bass walk-ups; big choir in parallel block harmony; call and response between leader and choir; a modulation up a half or whole step for the final chorus.
- Hammond organ with Leslie, piano, tambourine, hand claps on 2 and 4.
- Voice: full chest, melisma, ad-libs; the leader improvises over the choir's refrain.

## Country and American folk

- Major keys, I - IV - V and I - V - vi - IV; a 3/4 waltz for heartbreak, 4/4 train beat for the road; 70 to 110 BPM.
- Instruments: acoustic guitar strumming, pedal steel (the sound of longing), fiddle, banjo (brightness), dobro, upright bass, brushed snare.
- Voice: plain, nasal-warm, story-first, slight twang; the chorus states the point plainly. A text that praises someone directly ("none special like you") is native here.
- Appalachian ballad: modal (Dorian, Mixolydian), unaccompanied or with a single fiddle drone; the old lament voice.

## Form for a song

Prelude: ambience (rain on a window, a bar's murmur, crickets), one instrument alone (piano, slide guitar, pedal steel), narrator speaks like a late-night radio host or a porch storyteller. Verse: the text in 12-bar, AABA, or verse-chorus. Conclusion: a tag (last line repeated), ritardando, final chord with an added 6th or a bare fifth.

## Fusion partners

Pedal steel under a Carnatic violin (both slide); a gospel choir answering a qawwali leader; brushed drums under a kora; a blues harmonica with a shakuhachi's breath.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Straight eighths in swing; the triplet feel is the idiom.
- Blues without bends.
- Gospel without the choir's answer.
- Country with ironic distance; it only works sincere.
