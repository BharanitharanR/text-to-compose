# Hindustani (North Indian classical) and its lighter forms

Reach for it: Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Bengali text; night, longing, separation, monsoon, romance, Sufi devotion, a lamp-lit room.

## Raga by time and mood

Hindustani ragas carry a time of day. Match it to the backdrop's hour when you can; it is part of the immersion.

| Raga | Scale (Sa=C) | Time | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yaman | C D E F# G A B | early night | serene longing, the classic first raga |
| Bhairav | C Db E F G Ab B | dawn | austere devotion, awakening |
| Bhairavi | C Db Eb F G Ab Bb | morning, and as a closer any time | tenderness, farewell, sweetness in sorrow |
| Darbari Kanada | C D Eb F G Ab Bb, slow oscillating Eb and Ab | late night | gravity, majesty, grief |
| Malkauns | C Eb F Ab Bb | midnight | depth, meditation, something ancient |
| Desh | C D E F G A B asc / with Bb desc | late evening, monsoon | rain, patriotism, light romance |
| Bageshri | C D Eb F G A Bb | night | waiting for a lover |
| Kafi | C D Eb F G A Bb (Dorian) | spring, any | folk warmth, Holi, playfulness |
| Marwa | C Db E F# A B (no Pa) | sunset | unease, restlessness, the hour between |
| Pilu | mixed, Kafi-based | light forms | thumri, nostalgia, playful sorrow |

## Form

- **Alap**: slow, unmetered unfolding of the raga over the drone. Perfect prelude material; the narrator can speak over it.
- **Jor / jhala**: pulse enters, then fast strumming. Use for building energy before the verse.
- **Bandish or gat**: the composed piece with the words, in a tala.
- Lighter forms for lyric texts: **thumri** (romantic, expressive, free with the words), **ghazal** (couplets, refrain on the second line, intimate), **bhajan** (devotional, simple refrain), **qawwali** (Sufi, hand-claps, call-and-response, building ecstasy). A ghazal couplet structure often fits two-line user texts perfectly.

## Rhythm: tala

| Tala | Beats | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Teentaal | 16 (4x4) | default |
| Keherwa | 8 | bhajan, folk, light song |
| Dadra | 6 | thumri, ghazal, sway |
| Rupak | 7 (3+2+2) | lilt, unusual |
| Jhaptaal | 10 (2+3+2+3) | stately |
| Ektaal | 12 | slow, grave khayal |

## Ornament

**Meend** (long glide between notes), **andolan** (slow oscillation on a held note; mandatory in Darbari), **murki** (quick cluster), **kan** (grace note). Late-onset vibrato only. In the prompt: "Hindustani meend glides, no Western vibrato".

## Instruments

- Drone: **tanpura**; **harmonium** can double the voice and hold the drone in light forms.
- Melody: **sitar**, **sarod**, **bansuri** (bamboo flute, night and Krishna), **sarangi** (bowed, closest to the human voice; grief), **santoor** (hammered, water and Kashmir), **shehnai** (double reed, weddings and dawn).
- Rhythm: **tabla** (always), **pakhawaj** (older, deeper, for dhrupad gravity), **dholak** (folk, bhajan, qawwali).

## Fusion partners

Sarangi with a string quartet; bansuri over a soft synth pad; tabla under a Celtic reel at the same tempo; a ghazal refrain answered by a muted trumpet. Keep the drone; drop it and the world evaporates.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Changing chords under the melody. Hold the drone.
- Putting Bhairav at night or Yaman at dawn when the backdrop names the hour.
- Fast immediate vibrato.
- A drum kit instead of tabla.
- Forgetting the raga's forbidden notes (Marwa has no Pa; Malkauns has no Re or Pa). State the scale and stay in it.
