# Flamenco (Andalusia)

Reach for it: passion, pride, defiance, sorrow that is sung at full voice, heat, a courtyard at night, hands clapping, a text with short fierce lines.

## Harmonic and melodic logic

- **Phrygian mode with a major tonic chord**: in E, the scale is E F G A B C D, and the tonic chord is E major (the raised G#). The "Andalusian cadence" Am - G - F - E is the signature movement; everything resolves down to E.
- Melodies descend; the tension lives in the F to E half step.
- Cante uses microtonal bends and a cracked, strained attack on peak notes.

## Palos (forms) by mood

| Palo | Compás (cycle) | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Soleá | 12 beats, accents on 3 6 8 10 12 | solitude, dignity, grief: the mother form |
| Siguiriya | 12 (felt 5-beat: 1 2 3 4 5 with long 3 and 5) | deepest sorrow, death |
| Bulería | 12, fast, accents 3 6 8 10 12 | fire, party, defiance, humour |
| Alegría | 12, bright, in major | joy, the sea at Cádiz |
| Tangos | 4/4 | earthy, flirtatious, driving |
| Fandango | 3/4, free verse form | folk sweetness |
| Taranta / minera | free rhythm | mines, darkness, no compás |
| Sevillanas | 3/4 | festival dance |

Tempo: soleá 60 to 90, bulería 180 to 240, siguiriya very slow and free-feeling.

## Instruments and sounds

- **Flamenco guitar**: rasgueado (strumming rolls), golpe (tapping the body), picado runs, falsetas (melodic interludes between verses). Carries harmony and rhythm.
- **Palmas**: handclaps, sordas (muffled) and claras (sharp), in interlocking patterns. Essential.
- **Cajón**: box drum, modern but standard.
- **Jaleo**: shouts of encouragement ("olé", "eso es") between phrases.
- Footwork (zapateado) as percussion when a dancer is in the scene.
- Ambience: a stone courtyard, cicadas, a distant church bell, chairs scraping, a crowd murmuring then silent.

## Singing (cante)

Raw, powerful chest voice with a strained, cracked edge; long melisma on "ay" before the verse (the temple or salida); verses (letras) of three or four short lines; the singer bends into notes and leaves them with a sob. Prompt: "flamenco cante jondo, raw cracked voice, long melisma on ay". Spanish is native; another language works if the lines are short.

## Form

Prelude: guitar falseta alone over the Andalusian cadence, palmas sordas enter, narrator speaks between phrases. The singer's "ay" salida opens the verse. Verse: letras with guitar answering. Conclusion: a final descending phrase to the E major chord, one golpe, silence; or a short bulería escape ending on a shout.

## Fusion partners

Flamenco guitar with Carnatic violin (both Phrygian-adjacent); cajón under tabla; cante over a Hindustani Bhairav drone (both use the flat second).

## What outsiders get wrong

- Using harmonic minor instead of Phrygian-major.
- Steady 4/4 claps; the 12-beat compás and its accents are the identity.
- A clean, sweet voice. Cante is supposed to hurt a little.
