# Japanese traditional and traditional-influenced

Reach for it: stillness, seasons (cherry blossom, autumn maple, first snow), ceremony, a garden, restraint, a single gesture carrying great weight, silence as part of the music.

## Scales

| Scale | Notes (from D) | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| In (miyako-bushi) | D Eb G A Bb | the classic "Japanese" sound; melancholy, night, koto |
| Yo | D E G A B | bright, folk, festival, open |
| Ritsu | D E G A B (with C) | court music, calm |
| Hirajoshi | D Eb G A Bb (koto tuning) | elegant sorrow |
| Kumoi | D Eb G A B | variant, slightly brighter |

Ma (negative space) is structural: rests are as composed as notes. Leave silence between phrases, especially in the prelude and conclusion.

## Rhythm and form

- Free rhythm (shakuhachi honkyoku) follows the breath: one phrase per exhalation, then silence. Mark the score "free, by breath".
- Metered folk (min'yō) in 2/4 at a walking pace, with taiko and hand claps.
- **Jo-ha-kyū**: begin slowly and sparsely, intensify, then finish quickly and release. Map it to prelude (jo), verse (ha), conclusion (kyū).

## Instruments

- **Shakuhachi**: bamboo flute; breath noise is part of the tone; pitch bends by head movement (meri/kari). The loneliest sound in the palette. Prompt: "shakuhachi with audible breath, bending pitches".
- **Koto**: 13-string zither, arpeggiated patterns, bends by pressing strings; water and gardens.
- **Shamisen**: three-string plucked lute, percussive attack; narrative, urban, Tsugaru style is fast and driving.
- **Biwa**: lute for epic storytelling, dramatic buzzes.
- **Taiko**: drums, from a soft pulse to thunder; use the big sound sparingly.
- **Sho** (mouth organ, sustained clusters) and **hichiriki** (piercing double reed) for a court-music, otherworldly prelude.
- Small percussion: **kane** bell, wooden clappers.
- Ambience: a temple bell with long decay, wind in bamboo, rain on a roof, a stream, cicadas in summer, snow silence in winter.

## Singing

Narrow range, little vibrato, slightly nasal or very pure tone; long held notes with slow swell. Folk min'yō uses melismatic "kobushi" ornaments. For enka (modern popular sentiment) add heavy vibrato on sustained notes.

## Fusion partners

Shakuhachi over a cello drone; koto arpeggios with a Celtic harp; taiko under an orchestral swell; sho cluster under a Hindustani alap.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Filling every bar. Leave ma.
- The "Oriental riff" cliché (pentatonic parallel fourths). Avoid it entirely.
- Loud taiko throughout; taiko is a climax, not a bed.
- Mixing court (gagaku), folk and modern idioms without reason; choose one as the base.
