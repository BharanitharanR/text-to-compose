# Western orchestral and film scoring

Reach for it: cinema, romance, tension, grandeur, a sweeping landscape, an elegy, any text whose backdrop is a scene from a film. Also the glue for most fusions.

## Harmony by mood

| Device | Mood |
|---|---|
| Major with added 9ths and 6ths (Lydian colour: raised 4th) | wonder, flight, hope |
| Natural minor, slow I - VI - III - VII | yearning, epic sorrow |
| Dorian minor | bittersweet, journey |
| Pedal tone under shifting chords | suspense, awe |
| Chromatic mediants (C major to Ab major, or to E major) | magic, the uncanny, a reveal |
| Suspended chords unresolved | waiting, ambiguity |
| Cluster strings, sul ponticello, col legno | fear, danger |
| Plagal cadence (IV - I) | benediction, homecoming, the end |

## Orchestration by section

- **Strings**: the emotional core. Sustained pads (divisi, soft), ostinati (motion), tremolo (tension), pizzicato (lightness), solo violin or cello for the intimate voice.
- **Woodwinds**: oboe for sorrow and pastoral, flute for air and light, clarinet for warmth, bassoon for earth and humour, cor anglais for elegy.
- **Brass**: horns for nobility and distance, trumpets for announcement, trombones and tuba for weight and threat. Use late; brass is a climax.
- **Percussion**: timpani rolls for arrival, bass drum for dread, snare for march, cymbal swells for lift, glockenspiel and celesta for wonder, chimes for ceremony.
- **Harp and piano**: arpeggios for water and memory; a lone piano for the most private moments.
- **Choir**: wordless "ah" for sacred scale; a children's choir for innocence.

## Film-score techniques

- **Leitmotif**: one short motif tied to the text's subject, returning transformed (inverted, augmented, re-harmonised) in the conclusion.
- **Ostinato plus long melody**: motion underneath, a slow line above.
- **Swell and cut**: build with a crescendo then stop everything for one bar of silence before the turn line.
- **Spotting the turn**: the orchestra thins to a single instrument under the turn word, then re-enters.
- **Pedal to resolution**: hold a bass pedal through the verse, resolve it only at the conclusion.

## Tempo guide

Elegy 50 to 66; romantic theme 66 to 84; journey 90 to 110; action and anthem 120 to 150.

## Voice in an orchestral setting

Lyric soprano or baritone with classical vibrato for the grand version; a plain, pop-folk voice over orchestra for intimacy (the film-ballad sound); a boy soprano or choir for sacred texts.

## Fusion partners

Nearly anything: a tanpura drone under strings, a morin khuur over a string pad, a kora with harp, duduk over strings for the Middle East or Caucasus, a shakuhachi before a swell. The orchestra is the frame; let the fused instrument carry the melody or the prelude so it is not decoration.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Everything at full force. Dynamics are the drama; mark pp to ff and mean it.
- Brass from the start.
- Forgetting the silence before the turn.
- A motif that never returns.
