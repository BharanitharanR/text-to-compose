# Andean (Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, northern Chile and Argentina)

Reach for it: mountains, thin air, community and festival, a long walk, markets, the condor, resilience with a smile, sorrow that keeps dancing.

## Melodic logic

- Pentatonic minor is the backbone (A C D E G), often with a major-relative lift (C D E G A) for choruses. Many melodies alternate minor verse and relative-major chorus.
- Parallel thirds and fifths between two flutes or two voices are characteristic.
- Phrases descend to the tonic at the end; short, repeated, singable.

## Rhythms and forms

| Form | Meter | Tempo | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| Huayno | 2/4 with a lilting long-short feel | 100 to 130 | the core dance; joy with a melancholy edge |
| Yaraví | slow 3/4 or 6/8 | 55 to 75 | lament, love lost, the prelude |
| Carnavalito | 2/4 driving | 120 to 150 | festival, crowds |
| Sanjuanito (Ecuador) | 2/4 | 110 to 130 | nostalgic dance |
| Sikuri / sikuriada | 2/4, interlocking | 90 to 120 | panpipe ensembles playing hocket |

A huayno often opens with a slow free introduction (fuga comes at the end: a faster coda). This maps well to prelude (slow), verse (huayno), conclusion (fuga, then one long held flute note).

## Instruments

- **Zampoña / siku** (panpipes): two halves played by two people in hocket (alternating notes). Breathy, chordal when several play. Prompt: "Andean panpipes in hocket".
- **Quena**: notched end-blown flute; bright and plaintive, the solo voice.
- **Charango**: small 10-string lute (armadillo-shell history), fast strumming, bright shimmer.
- **Guitar**: bass lines and strummed rhythm.
- **Bombo legüero**: large bass drum with a soft mallet, the heartbeat.
- **Chajchas** (goat-hoof rattles), **tarka** (wooden flute, buzzy, carnival).
- Ambience: wind at altitude, a market, church bells, llamas and bells, a stream, fireworks at a fiesta.

## Singing

High, bright, slightly nasal chest voice, often in parallel thirds with a second voice. Spanish, Quechua or Aymara. Little vibrato; strong rhythmic diction.

## Fusion partners

Quena with Celtic low whistle; charango with a Hindustani santoor; panpipes over a synth pad; bombo with tabla.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Making it uniformly cheerful; the minor-pentatonic ache is the point.
- Panpipes as a solo "mystical" sound; they are a community instrument.
- Forgetting the strum: charango and guitar drive the huayno.
