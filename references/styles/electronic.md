# Electronic and ambient

Reach for it: abstract or futuristic worlds, machines, cities at night, dreams, the inside of a thought, a narrator that is an AI or a ghost in the system, any text where the backdrop has no acoustic room to put instruments in.

## Palettes by mood

| Palette | Sounds | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | slow-evolving pads, granular textures, field recordings, reverb tails longer than the notes | stillness, memory, space |
| Downtempo / trip-hop | 70 to 95 BPM, dusty drum breaks, sub bass, Rhodes, vinyl crackle | night city, melancholy with groove |
| Synthwave | 85 to 118 BPM, gated reverb snare, arpeggiated saw bass, bright analog leads | nostalgia for a future that never came |
| Techno / industrial | 120 to 140, four-on-the-floor kick, metallic percussion, noise sweeps | machines, pressure, ritual |
| Drone | a single chord held for the whole piece with slow timbral motion | ceremony, grief, the sublime |
| Glitch | stuttered samples, bit-crushing, micro-edits | fracture, anxiety, playfulness |
| Cinematic hybrid | orchestral strings plus synth bass, braams, processed percussion | trailer scale, dread, arrival |

## Harmonic logic

Static or slow: one or two chords for a whole section, movement by filter and timbre rather than chord change. Suspended and add9 chords; minor 7ths; Lydian for wonder; Phrygian for threat. Modulate by a half step for unease, by a third for a reveal.

## Sound design as instrumentation

- **Pad**: the room the song lives in. Low-pass filter opening over the prelude is the simplest "entering the world" gesture.
- **Sub bass**: felt not heard; sine at 40 to 60 Hz on the tonic.
- **Arpeggiator**: motion without melody; set it to the motif's notes.
- **Granular** and **reversed** samples: memory, time running backwards.
- **Field recordings**: the backdrop literally (rain, a station, a kitchen), pitched or looped.
- **Vocoder / formant shift**: the narrator as a machine; **tape delay** and **pitch drift**: the narrator as a memory.
- **Side-chain pumping**: the pad ducking to a kick, the breath of a city.

## Voice

Anything goes, which is why the choice must be explicit: a dry, close, whispered voice; a heavily reverbed distant voice; a vocoded machine; a human voice chopped into a sample. Pitch-corrected and layered octaves for a pop-electronic chorus. Keep the lyrics intelligible on the turn line even if nowhere else.

## Form

Prelude: a filtered pad opening slowly, a field recording of the backdrop, the narrator processed to fit (vocoder for a machine, tape warble for memory). Verse: the beat enters at the first sung line, or never (ambient). Build by adding layers every 8 bars. Conclusion: strip layers in reverse, leave the pad and the field recording, let the reverb tail end the piece.

## Fusion partners

The most natural fusion host: a tanpura drone is already a pad; a morin khuur over sub bass; tabla cut into a breakbeat; a shakuhachi phrase granularised into the pad; a kora loop as the arpeggiator.

## What outsiders get wrong

- Generic "epic" risers and braams without a world behind them.
- Burying the turn line in effects.
- Busy drums under a text that is about stillness.
